Firebase vs custom backend: what powers your app?

Both get real products to market. The right choice depends on what your app needs to do a year from now, not just at launch.

PSBM Labs · Updated 2026 · 6 min read

Shreyasi Madhu Co-Founder & Engineer, PSBM Labs

Firebase and a custom backend both power real, successful products — this isn't a question of one being objectively better. It's about matching the choice to your app's specific needs around speed, cost, and control.

When Firebase is the right call

  • Speed to MVP matters most — Firebase's authentication, real-time database, and hosting let a small team ship a working product fast.
  • You don't want to manage servers — Firebase handles infrastructure scaling and maintenance, which is one less thing a small team needs to own.
  • Real-time features are core to the app — chat, live updates, and collaborative features are genuinely easy to build on Firebase's real-time database.
  • Budget favors usage-based pricing early on — you pay for what you use, which is friendly for a pre-revenue product with low initial traffic.

When a custom backend is the right call

  • Complex business logic — workflows with many interdependent rules are usually cleaner to build and test in a traditional backend framework.
  • Cost predictability at scale — Firebase's usage-based pricing can become expensive and harder to predict as usage grows significantly.
  • Data portability and control — a custom backend gives you full control over your database and infrastructure, without being tied to one vendor's ecosystem.
  • Heavy, complex queries — relational data with complicated joins and reporting is often more natural in a traditional SQL-based backend than Firebase's document model.

A common, practical middle ground

We often start MVPs on Firebase to validate the product quickly, with an architecture that keeps business logic decoupled enough to migrate to a custom backend later if the product needs it. This isn't the right call for every project, but it's a reasonable way to de-risk an early-stage build without over-engineering from day one.

The question that actually decides it

Ask yourself: is my main constraint speed to launch, or long-term cost and control at scale? Firebase optimizes for the first; a custom backend optimizes for the second. Most projects lean clearly one way once that question is asked directly.
